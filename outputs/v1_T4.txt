## 1. Summary
TCP and UDP are the two primary transport layer protocols with distinct operational characteristics. TCP is connection-oriented, prioritizing reliable and ordered data delivery through mechanisms like handshakes and acknowledgments. Conversely, UDP is connectionless and prioritizes speed and low latency over reliability, making it ideal for real-time applications.

## 2. Key Concepts
*   TCP uses a three-way handshake to establish connections.
*   TCP ensures data integrity via acknowledgments and retransmissions.
*   TCP includes flow and congestion control mechanisms.
*   UDP operates without connection setup, reducing overhead.
*   UDP does not guarantee delivery, ordering, or duplicate protection.

## 3. Key Definitions
| Term | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| TCP | A connection-oriented protocol ensuring reliable, ordered data delivery. |
| UDP | A connectionless protocol prioritizing low latency over reliability. |
| Three-way Handshake | The process used by TCP to establish a connection. |
| Flow Control | A TCP mechanism to manage data transmission rates. |

1. (b) TCP

**Short-Answer:**
1. UDP is faster because it is connectionless and lacks the overhead of handshakes and reliability checks.
2. TCP uses acknowledgments and retransmissions to ensure data is received correctly.
3. Choose UDP when speed and low latency are more important than guaranteed delivery, such as in live streaming.
