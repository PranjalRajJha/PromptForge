## 1. Summary
An n-gram is a contiguous sequence of $n$ tokens used in language modeling to predict the next word in a sequence. By calculating probabilities based on the previous $n-1$ words, these models estimate the likelihood of word sequences using training corpus counts. Because unseen sequences result in zero probability, smoothing techniques like Laplace smoothing are applied to ensure all n-grams have a non-zero probability.

## 2. Key Concepts
*   **N-gram:** A contiguous sequence of $n$ tokens.
*   **Unigram/Bigram/Trigram:** Sequences of length 1, 2, and 3 respectively.
*   **Language Modeling:** Estimating the probability of a word based on preceding context.
*   **Training Corpus:** The dataset used to count occurrences for probability estimation.
*   **Smoothing:** A method to assign non-zero probabilities to unseen n-grams.

## 3. Key Definitions
| Term | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| N-gram | A contiguous sequence of $n$ tokens from a text. |
| Bigram Model | A model estimating $P(w_i \| w_{i-1})$. |
| Laplace Smoothing | A technique to assign small non-zero probabilities to unseen n-grams. |
